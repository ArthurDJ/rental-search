# Verification

Every listing in a hand-over has been opened on the day of the report. This file covers how to tell that a listing is live, how to merge duplicates, how to spot contradictions and scams, and how to treat lists that someone else made.

## Is it still listed

Open the listing's own page; a search card can outlive the ad. Signs that a listing is gone:

| Sign | Example |
|---|---|
| A removed-ad banner | Kijiji "No Longer Available"; liv.rent "marked as rented" |
| No price and a notify button | Zumper "Monthly rent –" with "Notify me" |
| A 404 or "Page Not Found" | Check the URL form first (`references/sites.md` → Craigslist) |
| The landlord says so in an edit | "RENTED" in the title |

A listing whose page says "available now" but whose status says rented is gone. Note the evidence in the gone list, for example "liv.rent shows rented, 2026-11-06".

Also record changes since an earlier check: price drops or rises, new availability dates, and relistings.

## Duplicates

The same unit is often on several sites, sometimes at different prices, and sometimes posted by both the landlord and a tenant who wants to sublet.

- Signs: the same address or unit number, the same photos, the same size and price, the same unusual wording.
- Keep one row with every link, and the lowest current price with its site. Say when the prices differ.
- Two different units in one building are two rows.
- When you suspect a match but cannot confirm it, keep both and add a note ("may be the same unit as …").

## Contradictions

Check the listing against itself before you trust it.

- **Building tags against unit facts.** A building page tagged "Furnished" while the building's own listing elsewhere says "Furnished: No" means **To confirm**.
- **Checkbox against description.** A "furnished" checkbox with a description that never mentions furniture, or "parking included" in one place and "parking $100/month" in another.
- **Status against text.** "Rented" in the status with "available immediately" in the description: treat as gone.
- **Old dates.** "Available November 1" read in late November or for a December move-in: ask whether the date still holds; the unit may be rented or the landlord may wait.
- **Occupancy.** "Best for a single tenant" or "one person only" when two people will live there: **Fails** if strict, **To confirm** if soft.

## Scam signals

One signal alone is common in honest ads. Two or more together, especially the first three, mean you should list the ad as a suspected scam with the reasons and keep it out of the recommendations.

1. The price is far below similar units in the same building or area (for example, under 60% of nearby asks for the same size).
2. The poster asks to move off the platform at once ("check my profile for contact details", "email me only") or the platform marks the poster's ID as unverified.
3. Money before a viewing: a deposit "to hold" the unit, an application fee, or payment by wire transfer, gift card or cryptocurrency.
4. The landlord is "out of the country" and will mail the keys.
5. Photos that look like a hotel, a show suite or another listing; text that does not match the photos.
6. Pressure: "many people interested, decide today".

Never contact the poster to test a suspicion. Tell the person what you saw and let them decide.

## Lists from someone else

A handoff note, an earlier session's table, another agent's shortlist or the person's own spreadsheet is a list of leads.

- Re-open every listing before carrying it forward.
- Check claims you can check: the commute (measure it), the address (find it on the page), "all utilities included" (read it).
- When something on the list turns out wrong, say so plainly in a "corrections" note, so the person can update whatever else relied on it.
