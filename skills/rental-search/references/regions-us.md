## Seattle, Washington

Sources: RCW 59.18 on app.leg.wa.gov, seattle.gov (Renting in Seattle, SDCI, Office for Civil Rights).

- **Deposits need a written lease and a signed move-in checklist** (RCW 59.18.260). The deposit comes back within **30 days** of move-out with an itemized statement (RCW 59.18.280).
- **Seattle cap**: security deposit plus non-refundable move-in fees together at most **one month's rent**; move-in fees at most **10%** of the first month's rent, only for screening and cleaning; a pet deposit up to **25%** of one month's rent on top (SMC 7.24.035, 7.24.038).
- **Installments**: the tenant may pay the deposit, fees and last month's rent in installments (6 payments on a lease of 6 months or more, 4 on 30 days to 6 months, 2 on month-to-month), with no fee for choosing to.
- **First-in-time**: landlords post screening criteria and offer the unit to the first qualified complete application (SMC 14.08.050).
- **Non-refundable fees** must be called non-refundable in a written lease, or they count as a refundable deposit (RCW 59.18.285).
- **Rent increases**: none in the first 12 months; after that capped at the lesser of 7% plus inflation or 10% a year (9.683% for 2026), with 90 days' notice; buildings 12 years old or newer are exempt (RCW 59.18.700, .710, from 2025-05-07).
- Short-term rentals (under 30 nights) need a city license number (format STR-OPLI-…) on the listing (SMC 6.600).

## California: San Francisco Bay Area and Los Angeles

Sources: Civil Code on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, oag.ca.gov, sfplanning.org, housing.lacity.gov.

- **Deposit cap**: at most **one month's rent**, furnished or not, for deposits demanded since 2024-07-01; "security" includes move-in fees and advance rent, and no deposit may be called non-refundable (Civil Code 1950.5). Small landlords (individuals or all-individual LLCs owning at most 2 rental properties with at most 4 units) may take up to two months.
- **Pet deposits** count inside the same cap (no separate allowance).
- **Return**: within **21 days** of move-out, itemized, with receipts above $125 and move-in and move-out photos (1950.5).
- **Screening fee**: actual cost only, capped at $30 per applicant adjusted for inflation since 1998; itemized receipt required (1950.6). The current dollar figure is not published on an official page.
- **Payment method**: the landlord must accept at least one way to pay that is neither cash nor electronic transfer, such as a check (1947.3). A landlord who insists on cash, wire or app payment only is breaking the law, and it is a scam signal.
- **Rent increases (AB 1482)**: the starting rent is free; later increases capped at 5% plus regional inflation or 10%, whichever is lower; many single-family homes, condos and buildings under 15 years old are exempt (1947.12). San Francisco and Los Angeles rent-controlled units have stricter local caps.
- **Short-term rentals**: San Francisco requires a registration number on listings under 30 days; Los Angeles bans home-sharing in rent-stabilized (RSO) units and requires a registration number elsewhere.

## New York City, New York

Sources: General Obligations Law and Real Property Law on nysenate.gov, nyc.gov (DCWP, Office of Special Enforcement), hcr.ny.gov, ag.ny.gov.

- **Deposit cap**: at most **one month's rent**, returned within **14 days** of move-out with an itemized statement (GOL 7-108).
- **Application fees**: no fee to process an application; background and credit checks at most **$20** in total, with the report and receipt given to the applicant (RPL 238-a).
- **Broker fees (FARE Act, from 2025-06-11)**: a broker hired by the landlord, or listing the unit with the landlord's permission, cannot charge the tenant; all fees the tenant must pay have to appear in the listing and in a signed written disclosure. A listing agent asking the tenant for a broker fee is a red flag.
- **Short-term rentals**: renting a whole apartment for under **30 days** is illegal; a legal short stay needs a registered host living in the unit (Local Law 18). For a furnished stay, plan on 30 days or more.
- **Rent-stabilized units**: the lease comes with a rider showing how the rent was set; the tenant can request the unit's rent history from the state housing agency (DHCR).
- **Pets**: in buildings with 3 or more units, a no-pet clause is waived if a pet is kept openly for 3 months and the owner knows and does nothing (NYC Admin Code 27-2009.1).

## Austin, Texas

Sources: Texas Property Code ch. 92 on statutes.capitol.texas.gov, austintexas.gov.

- **No legal cap on the deposit amount**. The deposit comes back within **30 days** of move-out, itemized, once the tenant gives a written forwarding address (Property Code 92.103, 92.107).
- **Application fee**: the landlord must make written selection criteria available; if not, and the applicant is rejected, the fee is refunded (92.351–92.354).
- No statewide rent control.
- **Short-term rentals** under 30 days need a city license number on the listing.
- Do not apply California or New York limits here; quote Texas rules only.
