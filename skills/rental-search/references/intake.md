# Intake

Ask in one short round, in plain words, in the person's language: at most 5 questions, the ones that change the result most (usually budget, move-in, the commute anchor and limit, the household and the unit type). Write each as one sentence with one question mark; when points belong together, fold them into that sentence ("Where does she work, and how and when does she travel there?" counts as one) instead of adding sub-questions. The first round has at most 5 question marks. Ask module questions and the rest later, when they matter. Skip what the message already answers. For each question you skip because it does not change the result, state the default you are using. A missing budget, move-in date or pet answer stays open: do not fill it in, and let the table show the gap.

## Base questions

| Field | Ask | Why it matters | If unanswered |
|---|---|---|---|
| City and area | Which city? Any areas you want or want to avoid? | Picks the region pack and the sites | Required: ask |
| Commute anchor | Where do you travel to most days (work, school)? By car, transit, bike or on foot? What time do you leave? | The commute is measured, not guessed | Ask; if there is none, skip commute scoring |
| Commute limit | What is the longest commute you will accept, one way? | Turns the commute into a filter | Show times, no filter |
| Budget | Monthly rent you aim for, and the most you would pay | Price is usually the heaviest weight | Leave open; sort by price, no cut-off |
| Move-in | When do you want to move in? How flexible is it? | Units listed "available now" are often gone before a later date | Leave open; show each listing's date |
| Lease length | 1 year, 6 months, month-to-month, a fixed number of months? | Filters out leases that do not fit | Show each listing's terms |
| Who lives there | How many adults and children? | Some listings cap occupancy or rent to one person | Ask if more than one person is implied |
| Unit type | Room, studio, 1 bedroom, 2 bedrooms or more? Is a studio acceptable? | Hard filter on size | Accept all whole units |
| Must-haves | In-suite laundry, parking, anything you will not live without? | Hard filters vs nice-to-haves | Treat as nice-to-have |

## Requirement modules

Turn a module on when the message mentions its topic, or when the person says yes to it. Each module adds questions, columns in the table and points in the score. Keep the modules that are off out of the table.

### Pets

- Ask: what animals, how many, size or weight, and breed if dogs.
- Check on each listing: allowed, "on approval", or not allowed; weight or breed limits; pet deposit and monthly pet fee; strata or building rules if it is a condo.
- Treat "pets negotiable" or "on approval" as **To confirm**, not as yes.
- Columns: Pets allowed, Pet fees.

### Business or relocation stay

For people on a work assignment, a relocation, or a stay that a company pays for.

- Ask: dates and length (often 1–6 months); who pays (the person, then reimbursed, or the company directly); what the company needs (receipts, an invoice with the landlord's name and address, a lease in the company's name, insurance certificate); work needs (desk, internet speed, quiet); distance to the airport or the office.
- Check on each listing: will the landlord issue receipts or invoices; accepts company payment or a corporate lease; minimum stay; whether utilities and internet are included (reimbursement is easier when they are); furnished with a work surface.
- Columns: Receipts or invoice, Minimum stay, Internet.

### Furnished

- Ask: fully furnished, or are basics enough? Do you need kitchenware and bedding?
- Check: what the furniture actually is. "Furnished" can mean a full home, or a sofa and a bed. Note "kitchen cabinets empty", "no linens" and similar.
- A building or search-page tag "furnished" is a lead: confirm it on the unit's own page (`references/verification.md` → Contradictions).
- Columns: Furniture.

### Parking and EV

- Ask: how many cars? Is street parking acceptable? Do you need EV charging?
- Check: stalls included or extra per month; covered or surface; street parking rules; EV charging.
- No parking at all is a hard fail when the person has a car and street parking is not acceptable.

### Family and children

- Ask: children's ages; school catchment that matters; need for a yard, playground or daycare nearby.
- Check: bedrooms, school catchment (from the school district's lookup, not the listing), nearby parks.

### Accessibility

- Ask: steps, elevator, ground floor, door widths, accessible bathroom.
- Check: the listing's own words and photos; mark anything not visible as **To confirm**.

### Shared housing and roommates

- Ask: whether a room in a shared home is acceptable, and who they would share with.
- Check: private or shared bathroom, who else lives there, rules (guests, quiet hours), whether the lease is with the landlord or a tenant (sublet).

### Short and mid-term stays

- Ask: exact dates; whether a sublet or lease takeover is acceptable.
- Check: whether the landlord permits a sublet; the end date of the original lease; furnished; whether utilities are included.

### Other needs

Smoking, storage, quiet, a specific building, a top floor, a view, a gym, wheelchair access for visitors and similar: write the need down in the person's words, add it as a nice-to-have column, and mark each listing yes, no or not stated.

## Defaults to state

When you assume something, say it in one line, for example: "No budget given, so I am sorting by price without a cut-off" or "Commute measured by car, leaving 8:15 on a weekday".
