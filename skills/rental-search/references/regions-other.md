# Regions without a pack

Packs exist for British Columbia (`references/regions-canada-bc.md`), Ontario (`references/regions-canada-on.md`) and five US metro areas (`references/regions-us.md`). Everywhere else, the general workflow still applies, but the list of sites and the local rules have to be worked out first, and the person should know that.

## No pack yet

1. **Find the local sites.** Start with the national sites in `references/sites.md` that serve the country, then use a web search for the city's own rental sites, local classifieds and furnished or mid-term rental sites. Prefer sites that real renters there use over aggregators that copy listings.
2. **Show the list to the person before searching**, with one line per site on what it is good for, and ask whether to add or drop any. They often know a local site you will miss.
3. **Learn each new site as you go.** Record what you learn in the shape of `references/sites.md`: search URL pattern, whether filters work, what a removed listing looks like, where the address or coordinates are, what needs a sign-in.
4. **Look up the local tenancy rules** that change the decision (deposit limits, fees, short-stay rules) on official government pages, and cite them with the date. Do not carry rules over from another province or state.
5. **Label the results.** Say once at the top of the hand-over that this region has no pack, so coverage may be uneven.
6. **Keep every other rule**: open each listing, measure commutes, re-check status, screen for scams, never contact landlords.

## Writing a new pack

When a search in a new region goes well, offer to write a pack so the next search starts faster. Copy the structure of `references/regions-canada-bc.md`:

1. **Sites to search**: a table of the sites that work there, pointing to `references/sites.md` for how each behaves. Add a section to `references/sites.md` for any new site.
2. **Local facts**: tenancy rules that affect the decision, with the law or official page they come from and the date checked.
3. **Local notes**: commute patterns or areas, labeled with the date and the trip they were measured on.

Leave out the person's own details (names, workplace, budget, the specific listings). A pack describes the region and its sites, not one person's search.
